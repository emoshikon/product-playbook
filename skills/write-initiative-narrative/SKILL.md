# Write Initiative Narrative

Help the product owner express why an initiative matters and the world after it succeeds. An initiative has two canonical documents: a flexible narrative of lasting intent and structured living requirements that evolve as the team learns. They can reference supporting artifacts, such as mockups or API examples, that work with requirements to define finer details. Those details need not be copied into either document.

## Work with what the user brings

Start from the user's idea, notes, questions, or existing draft. Help explore who benefits, what lasting change they experience, why it matters, and what success means. Follow the uncertainty that matters to the user rather than running a fixed interview.

Choose an approach that helps the work: explore an idea, challenge an assumption, draft from notes, or revise existing prose. Preserve the user's useful language and chosen form. An announcement, blog post, customer story, or vision memo may help, but no template, format, or set of headings is required.

Make success concrete where the available evidence allows. Distinguish known facts from assumptions and proposed outcomes or measures. Keep consequential unknowns visible without forcing completeness or inventing targets.

## Keep lasting intent in focus

Keep features, interfaces, implementation choices, scope commitments, and exact delivery dates out of the enduring narrative. When these appear in source material, recover the purpose they serve and retain the details for requirements work. Do not silently discard them or turn them into approved commitments.

Check: if the team discovers a different way to achieve the outcome, does the narrative still hold? If it depends on a particular solution, clarify the underlying benefit with the user.

Evergreen means focusing on lasting intent, not forbidding revision. Changes to purpose, intended outcome, beneficiaries, or the meaning of success require an explicit product-owner decision. Keep proposed revisions distinct from agreed intent. A change in how the team achieves the outcome does not by itself require a narrative change.

## Connect to requirements

Save the narrative as `initiative-<initiative-name>.md`.

Keep the narrative separate and link it from the living requirements rather than duplicating it there. Link supporting context where useful without making the narrative depend on a particular solution. For detailed requirements, use `define-requirements` when available. Hand over collected requirement details, relevant artifact links, and unresolved questions without expanding this skill into requirements definition. When agreed narrative intent changes, flag affected requirements and supporting definitions for review.
